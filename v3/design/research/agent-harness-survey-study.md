# Study — "Agent Harness Engineering: A Survey" (the ETCLOVG taxonomy)

**Source:** Li, Xiao, Zhang, Liu, et al. (CMU / Yale / JHU / Northeastern / Tulane / UAB / OSU / Virginia Tech / Amazon), *Agent Harness Engineering: A Survey*, 2026. Companion page <https://picrew.github.io/LLM-Harness/>, OpenReview `eONq7FdiHa`, living catalog *Awesome-Agent-Harness*.
**Type:** survey / normative taxonomy paper — **not** a codebase. ~138 open-source projects coded against a 7-layer taxonomy from their *public artifacts* (README/docs/papers/release notes).
**Why it is in this series:** every other study reverse-engineers ONE system and maps its mechanisms onto v3 levels. This one is the inverse and the only one of its kind — an **external, independent decomposition of the whole problem space**. Its value is as a **checksum on v3's level map**: does an academic survey that never heard of pairflow carve the harness at the same joints we did (L0a..L14)? Where it agrees, our boundaries are not idiosyncratic. Where it disagrees, one of us is wrong — and the disagreements turn out to be the most informative part.

> Read this study as a **Rosetta stone + completeness audit**, not as a source of new mechanisms. It contributes no `file:line`; it contributes a vocabulary to check ours against, and a list of five field-wide open problems to check our open gaps against.

---

## Executive summary — seven load-bearing findings

1. **The thesis is our thesis, named from the outside.** The survey's framing — *"the harness is becoming the binding constraint; reliability depends less on the model than on the infrastructure layer that wraps it"* — and its headline arc, **"from agent frameworks (local abstractions: agents, tools, memory, loops) to agent platforms (durable workspaces, identity, observability, evaluation, governance, human handoff across many runs and many users)"** — is verbatim the positioning of v3 as a *kernel/platform, not a framework*. An independent academic group converged on the same frontier direction. This is the strongest external validation in the corpus that v3 is aimed at the recognized problem, not a private one.

2. **ETCLOVG ↔ v3 maps cleanly — except at exactly the two joints that are v3's contribution.** Six of the seven layers (E, T, C, L, V, G) map onto v3 levels with no friction (crosswalk in §3). The two mismatches are diagnostic: (a) the survey has **no name for the transactional execution kernel** — idempotency ledger, `expected_version` CAS, commit-vs-replay — it folds "control flow that reads and writes state" into one big **L (Lifecycle)** layer and treats durability as a *context/state-estimation* problem under open-problem #2; (b) the survey has **no first-class layer for channels/communication** — it disperses handoffs across T (protocol), L (orchestration), O (observability) and elevates them only as open-problem #4. **v3 separates both into their own levels (L0a kernel; L8 channels).** The survey under-weighting precisely v3's two deepest bets is evidence those bets are *non-obvious*, not that they are wrong.

3. **L is the densest layer in the open-source ecosystem (47 of ~138 primary projects) — and it is exactly v3's kernel territory.** Lifecycle & orchestration dwarfs every other layer (next is V=21, E=20). The field pours its effort into control loops, multi-agent patterns, and issue-to-PR pipelines — the same place v3 puts L0a/L0d/L4/L6. This says the *demand* for what v3 builds is real and crowded; the *differentiator* is not "do orchestration" but the kernel discipline (finding 2a) that the 47 projects mostly lack — the survey itself never mentions idempotency, which means the crowd is building L without L0a, exactly the **idempotency hole** this series has flagged in five studied systems.

4. **The `verify` gate is corroborated a FOURTH time, now from the meta-level.** V (Verification & Evaluation) is an *independent top-level layer* in ETCLOVG — co-equal with Execution and Lifecycle — with five subcategories (benchmark grounding, pre-execution readiness validation, controlled execution & trace capture, multi-level judgement & failure attribution, continuous regression feedback). After Superpowers (the principle), gastown's gate-bead (structural), and gstack (three-phase evidence), an academic survey independently promoting verification to a first-class layer makes `verify` the single most-validated addition in the entire research corpus — and tells us where it belongs: a *layer*, not a gate sub-type buried in L2.

5. **The three cross-layer patterns are direct design constraints — and v3 already answers two of them structurally.** (a) *Cost–quality–speed trilemma* → v3's **inline-vs-deferred gate axis** is literally the survey's prescription ("which checks can run asynchronously or in regression suites"). (b) *Capability–control tradeoff* → the survey says this is **ONE design axis** spanning "tool schemas, context policy, runtime permissions, identity, auditability, human approval" — v3 fragments it across L1/L2/L3/L7/L13; a real tension to weigh (§7). (c) *Harness coupling problem* → "test harness changes as system changes" = the verify-gate-as-system-test and Superpowers' whole-rollout discipline; a warning against optimizing any single level in isolation.

6. **All five field-wide open problems land on v3's open gaps — and #4 confirms L9 has no prior art anywhere.** Open-problem #4 ("standard handoffs that transfer not only a text summary but **intent, constraints, permissions, artifacts, provenance, budget state, risk level, trace history, and unresolved decisions**") is the richest external description yet of the L8/L9 context-packet-plus-correlation problem — and the survey poses it as *open*, confirming that fuzzy/external correlation and rich handoff are unsolved across the whole field, not just in the systems we happened to read. The survey's handoff wish-list is a near-complete spec for v3's ContextPacket; adopt it as a checklist.

7. **The survey's blind spots tell v3 what to keep loud — with one correction from the full paper.** Because the survey codes from *public READMEs* with *primary-label* assignments, it under-counts **Observability + Governance ("thinner in open source… operational control matured later")** — layers v3 must treat as first-class on principle rather than copy, because there is little to copy. *Correction (full-paper §5.5):* the companion-page reading "Context/memory C=9 because memory ships inside frameworks" is only half right — the *primary-label* count is low, but §5.5 documents a **rich standalone-memory market** (MemGPT, Mem0 at ~14M downloads, A-MEM, Honcho, cq as an open MCP-native standard, MemoryBank, Generative Agents + three memory benchmarks). So the honcho standalone-memory bet is **strengthened**, not merely validated: there is a real, named market for memory-as-a-separate-engine. The thin *primary* count is a coding artifact, not a market gap.

## 1. What the survey is, precisely

A taxonomy paper that (a) argues the harness is an *independent system layer*, (b) proposes the seven-layer **ETCLOVG** decomposition, and (c) codes a living corpus of ~138 open-source projects against it. Three explicit claims: harnesses are independent system layers; ETCLOVG separates production concerns earlier frameworks conflate; a broad ecosystem map reveals gaps. The corpus construction protocol (Fig. *construction_protocol*) is a pipeline: **Search Sources** (GitHub, arXiv/papers, curated lists, company blogs, package registries) → **Candidate Collection** (artifacts, public docs, deduplication) → **Inclusion Criteria** (public evidence, agentic workflow, harness mechanism) → **Boundary & Coding** (boundary check, layer mapping, evidence notes). Coding is **multi-label** (one primary layer = the most central mechanism; secondary layers only when public docs expose an independent capability).

This methodology is the study's biggest caveat (§ Caveats): it measures *what projects advertise in their README*, not what they do or how well. It is a map of the *published* ecosystem, weighted toward what is open-sourced and documented.

## 2. The ETCLOVG taxonomy (full subcategory tree, from Fig. *taxonomy_tree*)

> First four layers (E, T, C, L) = the **structural core** of a harness. Last three (O, V, G) = the **control plane** around it. Visual structure (Fig. *taxonomy*): **O** spans the top (system-wide monitoring); **E/T/C/L** are the four pillars; **V** runs underneath (evaluation/feedback); **G** is the foundation (governance across the whole system).

| Layer | Scope | Subcategories (the survey's section leaves) | Primary projects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E** — Execution Environment & Sandbox | where agent code runs, what bounds it | general-purpose managed sandbox · computer-use agent infrastructure · code-specialized runtimes · framework-integrated runtimes · browser evaluation environments · OS-level permission sandboxes · sandbox abstraction layers | **20** |
| **T** — Tool Interface & Protocol | how capabilities are described/discovered/invoked | protocol & interface standards (MCP, A2A) · tool description, discovery & selection · tool-augmented training & integration · scalability & session management | **12** |
| **C** — Context & Memory Management | what the model sees across horizons | short-term active context window · mid-term session state & cross-run persistence · long-term persistent memory systems · long-horizon context techniques · context drift & limits | **9** |
| **L** — Lifecycle & Orchestration | control flow that reads/writes state | single-agent inner loop · multi-agent orchestration patterns · full lifecycle task pipelines | **47** |
| **O** — Observability & Operations | traces, cost, failures, reliability | tracing & monitoring platforms · agent-specific observability platforms · cost tracking & optimization · reliability engineering · unified observability | **15** |
| **V** — Verification & Evaluation | tasks/traces → evaluation & feedback | task & benchmark grounding · pre-execution readiness validation · controlled execution & trace capture · multi-level judgement & failure attribution · continuous regression & deployment feedback | **21** |
| **G** — Governance & Security | constrain behavior model/system/org-level | permission models & identity management · lifecycle hooks · component hardening · declarative constitutions · audit infrastructure · agent security landscape | **14** |

The three engineering phases (Fig. *teaser*) frame the whole: **Prompt Engineering** (2022–2024, single-call, "optimize the model input") → **Context Engineering** (2025, multi-step, "optimize what the model sees") → **Harness Engineering** (2026–, system-level, "optimize how the model runs"). The teaser's right column *is* ETCLOVG in miniature: Tools & Environment, Orchestration, Verification & Evaluation, Governance & Security.

## 4. Per-layer reading — LEARN / AVOID / ORTHOGONAL

**E — Execution.** *LEARN:* the seven E subcategories are a ready-made menu for L0e's adapter surface (managed sandbox / microVM / code runtime / computer-use / browser / OS-permission / abstraction layer) — and "sandbox abstraction layers" being a named subcategory validates that L0e should be an *interface over* backends, the exact lesson gastown taught by rejecting one. The parked tmux topic is an **E concern (substrate) + a sliver of T (I/O transport) + the observe-seam (observation)** — the survey's E/T/O split is itself the disambiguation the parked doc was reaching for. *ORTHOGONAL:* microVM/desktop-VM cost models are below v3's abstraction line.

**T — Tool Interface & Protocol.** *LEARN:* MCP/A2A as *protocol standards* (not just integrations) supports treating the ActorAdapter as a protocol boundary. "Scalability & session management" as a T subcategory is the sticky-session/affinity concern Temporal taught — it belongs with the tool/adapter layer, not the kernel. *AVOID:* tool-augmented *training* is out of scope for an orchestration kernel.

**C — Context & Memory.** *LEARN:* the three horizons (short active window / mid-term session+cross-run / long-term persistent) are a cleaner naming for v3's L0b→L11 progression than v3 currently uses; adopt "horizon" as the axis label. "Context drift & limits" names the failure mode honcho's two-speed loop and the compaction work address. *Signal:* C=9 (the thinnest structural pillar) because memory ships *inside* frameworks — confirms honcho's standalone-memory-engine bet is genuinely rare and worth v3 treating as a first-class scope rather than an embedded afterthought.

**L — Lifecycle & Orchestration.** *LEARN:* the three-level decomposition (single-agent inner loop → multi-agent patterns → full issue-to-PR pipeline) is exactly v3's L0d→L4→WF-1 ladder; an independent group drawing the same three rungs validates the ladder. *AVOID / the core finding:* the survey treats this 47-project layer as *the* hard part while never naming the transactional kernel inside it — **the entire crowded L ecosystem is building orchestration on top of the idempotency hole.** v3's differentiator is not another orchestrator; it is L0a underneath one. Do not let the survey's framing (durability ⊂ context) bleed into v3 — keep L0a separate from C/L.

**O — Observability & Operations.** *LEARN:* the five O subcategories (tracing / agent-specific platforms / cost tracking / reliability engineering / unified observability) are a checklist v3's observe-seam under-serves; **cost tracking** in particular is absent from v3's level map and is the measurement side of the cost-quality-speed trilemma. *Reconsider:* whether the observe-seam should graduate to a named cross-cutting concern with cost/trace as explicit outputs (§7).

**V — Verification & Evaluation.** *LEARN:* the five V subcategories give the `verify` gate a sub-structure it lacked: **pre-execution readiness validation** (a *front* gate — gstack's CEO premise-gate fits here) vs **controlled execution & trace capture** vs **multi-level judgement & failure attribution** (the panel-of-judges pattern) vs **continuous regression feedback** (deferred gates → regression suite). Map v3's gate taxonomy onto these five; they are more complete than v3's current allow/warn/block × inline/deferred grid on the *evaluation* axis. *Trace-native:* "traces should be the primary object from which systems compute scores… not after-the-fact debugging material" (open-problem #3) = v3's commit-log-as-truth, applied to evaluation.

**G — Governance & Security.** *LEARN:* the six G subcategories validate v3's spread across L2/L3/L7/L10/L13 and add two names worth lifting: **declarative constitutions** (a policy-config artifact — v3 already has `v3-gate-policy-config-design-synthesis.md`; "constitution" is the right user-facing word) and **agent security landscape** (the L10/L13 adversarial frontier gastown's Wasteland/Spider gestured at). *Confirms:* "lifecycle hooks" as a G subcategory = gstack's PreToolUse hook belongs to governance, and v3's instinct to make it default-on/fail-closed is the production-grade version.

## 5. The three cross-layer patterns as v3 constraints

1. **Cost–quality–speed trilemma.** "Production harnesses cannot treat quality as a scalar objective; they must decide which risks justify expensive controls and which checks can run asynchronously or in regression suites." → v3's **inline gate** = the expensive synchronous control; v3's **deferred gate** = the async/regression check. The survey independently motivates the inline/deferred axis v3 already has. *Add:* a cost dimension to gate config so the trilemma is *decidable* per gate (gastown/honcho cost-awareness; the O-layer cost-tracking input).

2. **Capability–control tradeoff (ONE axis, per the survey).** "Capability and control are a single design axis spanning tool schemas, context policy, runtime permissions, identity, auditability, and human approval." → v3 decomposes this single axis across **L1 (tool schemas) · L2b (context policy) · L0e/L7 (runtime permissions/identity) · paperclip-style audit · L3 (human approval)**. *Tension to weigh:* the survey says it is one axis; v3 makes it five levels. Five levels = composability and independent evolution; one axis = a single coherent policy surface the user reasons about once. The resolution is probably a **unified policy-config view (the "constitution") that projects onto the five levels** — config is one axis, mechanism is five levels. Worth an explicit note in `approach.md`.

3. **Harness coupling problem.** "A prompt, tool, sandbox, verifier, or monitor may look beneficial in isolation while degrading the whole rollout… Harness changes should be tested as system changes." → direct support for **verify-gate-as-system-test** and against per-level local optimization. Implication for the research series itself: the per-level matrix in `_synthesis.md` is a *decomposition for understanding*, not a license to optimize levels independently — v3's integration tests must exercise whole rollouts (WF-1..WF-7), not just unit levels.

## 7. What v3 should reconsider from the survey

- **Promote Observability to a first-class cross-cutting concern with cost tracking as an explicit output.** v3 has the observe-seam (read side) but no named home for traces/cost/reliability as *operational outputs*. The survey's O layer (15 projects, 5 subcategories) argues this is owned by a different team in production — exactly the "different team / different stack" argument for making it a boundary. **Action:** add an O concern to the level map (or formally bless the observe-seam as that concern) and add a **cost** field to make the trilemma decidable.
- **Adopt "horizon" as the C axis label and "constitution" as the policy-config label.** Naming hygiene that aligns v3 with the emerging field vocabulary at zero architectural cost.
- **Decide the capability–control axis-vs-levels question explicitly.** The survey's "one axis" framing is a real critique of v3's five-level spread. Resolution sketch: one *config surface* (constitution) projecting onto five *mechanism levels*. Write it down before it becomes accidental fragmentation.
- **Map the `verify` gate onto V's five subcategories.** v3's gate grid is rich on the *enforcement* axis (allow/warn/block × inline/deferred × declarative/packaged/process) but thin on the *evaluation* axis. V's five (benchmark-grounding / pre-exec-readiness / controlled-trace-capture / multi-level-judgement / continuous-regression) fill that out.
- **Keep L0a and L8 loud precisely because the survey is quiet on them.** The two things the external taxonomy under-weights are v3's two deepest bets. That is the signal to *lead* with them in v3's own framing — they are the non-obvious differentiators, not the table stakes.

## 9. Deep-dive addendum — mined from the full 50-page paper (5-subagent pass)

The companion page is a compression; the full paper (71 pp.) carries the mechanism-level detail. Five subagents read the paper in layer slices. The load-bearing additions and corrections, by layer:

### 9.1 E — Execution: the primary axis is *workload*, not isolation tech (§3)
- **Sandbox serves three purposes, and the agent-era novelty is the third: *liveness*.** A sandbox is not only a *cage* (security) and a *clean room* (reproducibility) but a **license to act without per-action human approval** — Anthropic reports **84% fewer permission prompts** in Claude Code once a sandbox bounds the blast radius (§3.1.2). This reframes v3's L0e: the runtime-context provider is the thing that **converts per-action governance into per-session configuration**. A direct tie between L0e and the L2/L3 gate-frequency problem.
- **The seven E subcategories are keyed on *workload* (managed / computer-use / code / framework-integrated / browser-eval / OS-permission / abstraction-layer); isolation primitive (container/gVisor/microVM/WASM/bubblewrap/Seatbelt) is an *orthogonal secondary property*** (§3.2). Sharpens this study's finding 4-E: L0e's adapter surface should be keyed on **workload type**, with isolation strength as a parameter — not the reverse.
- **"Sandbox abstraction layer" is a concrete, validated, named pattern = exactly L0e.** "Not a sandbox but an interface unifying multiple backends behind one API so the harness can switch substrates without rewriting agent code." Exemplars: **SWE-ReX** (Docker/Fargate/Modal/Daytona behind one API), **smolagents `executor_type`** (local/e2b/modal/docker/blaxel/wasm as one enum), **Kubernetes Agent Sandbox CRD** (declarative gVisor/Kata) (§3.2.7). The strongest external proof that L0e should be a named layer, not a per-actor feature. Design principle, verbatim: **"decouple what the agent runs from where it runs."**
- **Deployment is a clean 3-way: self-hosted / cloud-SaaS / hybrid-BYOC**, on axes latency × security × scalability + data-residency/compliance (§3.4). Hybrid = "agent logic and sandbox execution decoupled across environments" (OpenHands SDK Local/Remote Workspace). This **is** the parked agent-runtime topic's MVP-scope question (local-tmux vs headless/cloud) — adopt the Local/Remote-Workspace split as L0e's first-class abstraction.
- **The parked tmux topic, located precisely:** computer-use I/O (screen-observation + keystroke, via Xvfb/VM) is one *execution-substrate category* (§3.2.2), distinct from API/shell substrates. So the clean split the parked doc reached for is the paper's own E/T/O split: **substrate = E-category choice; I/O-transport (screen-scrape vs API vs shell) = a property of that category; observation = the O-seam.** tmux screen-scraping is a **low-fidelity point in the computer-use/shell I/O space — an adapter detail below v3's kernel line.** Confirmed, with the abstraction boundary now named.
- **Sandbox-escape is real but an adapter contract, not a kernel concern.** SandboxEscapeBench reports **15–35% escape rates against Docker** by frontier models (§3.3); v3 should have L0e adapters *declare* their isolation guarantees, not have the leaderless kernel enforce them. Design rule: **"minimal, not maximally capable"** substrate (LLM-in-Sandbox) — least-capable substrate that satisfies the workload.

### 9.2 T — Tool/Protocol: four boundaries, not one (§4)
- **The organizing principle is *integration boundary crossed*, yielding FOUR distinct protocol planes** (§4.1, Table 1): Model↔Function (function-calling/JSON), **Agent↔Capability (MCP/OpenAPI** — runtime-to-tool decoupling), **Agent↔Agent (A2A/ACP/ANP** — cross-process delegation via Agent Cards), Agent↔Repo/env (**AGENTS.md** — version-controlled policy, Markdown wire). **Sharpens this study's crosswalk:** v3 likely needs **distinct adapter seams for actor-*invocation* (Agent↔Capability) vs inter-actor *delegation* (Agent↔Agent)** — one `ActorAdapter` should not do both. And `AGENTS.md` is itself a recognized first-class protocol boundary (the commit-based, version-controlled-policy seam — native to pairflow's ethos).
- **"Fewer but better tools" is empirically backed for L1.** Oversized menus degrade reliability, inflate tokens, amplify planning errors; selection must be **adaptive/per-step, not a static global list** (named methods: EasyTool, MCP-Zero, AnyTool, CRAFT, MetaTool, ToolRegistry, ToolRet) (§4.2). v3's L1 capability matrix should be **pruned per-step**, not just enumerated.
- **Skill-selection is a *distinct* retrieval problem from tool-selection** (SkillRouter, SkillRet retrieve procedural modules from large overlapping libraries) (§4.2) — validates L5 (skills) as a separate retrieval surface above L1 (tools).
- **Tool/reasoning returns should be evidence-bearing artifacts (traces, counterexamples, certificates), not black-box booleans** (§4.3) — a T-layer return-contract principle that feeds the V seam: adapters carry structured evidence.

### 9.3 C — Context/Memory: the conflation v3 exists to fix, located exactly (§5)
- **THE conflation, verbatim and located: §5.6 files durable-execution recovery *under context management*.** "Checkpoint and resume patterns allow agents to survive transient failures without losing task state" and "lifecycle hooks trigger compaction or memory consolidation" are presented together under **"Context management should be the infrastructure's job, not the agent's."** This lumps **checkpoint/resume (durable execution → v3 L0a kernel)** with **compaction/consolidation (memory → v3 L11)**. **v3's L0a-vs-L11 split is the precise correction; cite §5.6 as the thing v3 disambiguates.**
- **The state-estimation framing is explicit (§5.7):** context drift is "a harness property rather than a model property" (Bölük 2026b) — the model sees only "the projection of state exposed by the harness." The survey's drift *cure* is verification loops + HITL checkpoints + behavioral-divergence anomaly detection (pushed to O/G), **not a transactional ledger.** v3's commit-not-replay ledger is a different, stronger, deterministic answer — do **not** import the anomaly-detection-on-drift framing as v3's reliability mechanism.
- **Vocabulary to adopt:** **context *rot*** (single-step: more tokens degrade reasoning, begins well before window-full — a 200K model fails at 50K) vs **context *drift*** (trajectory-level: after 100+ turns agents repeat work, contradict earlier decisions, lose the goal; compaction summaries compound inaccuracy). Rot = the L0b packet-sizing constraint; drift = the L0b/L11 long-horizon failure (§5.1 vs §5.7).
- **Memory reference models for L11/L12:** MemGPT (**context-window-as-RAM, external-store-as-disk** paging) §5.5.1; Generative-Agents **observation→reflection→retrieval** triad (retrieval ranked by recency×importance×relevance; reflection = the slow loop) §5.5.1; **Honcho models *users not facts*, async "dreaming" = the slow loop, per-agent perspective prevents cross-contamination while a shared user-model accumulates** — names v3's `(observer, observed)` edge precisely §5.5.2; **MemoryBank** adds Ebbinghaus decay + contradiction resolution; **A-MEM** does *retroactive* link/attribute updates (significance is not write-time-fixed → argues mutable back-linkable memory edges); **cq** = collective fleet memory, MCP-native, 3 tiers (local/org/cross-team) + post-error hook auto-query — directly relevant to a leaderless multi-actor kernel §5.5.2.
- **KV-cache hit rate = "single most important metric for production agents"** (Manus: cached $0.30 vs uncached $3.00/MTok; one prefix-token change invalidates everything downstream). Hard constraint on **how an L0b context-packet is assembled: append-only, stable ordering, deterministic serialization** (§5.3). Compaction rule if v3 ever summarizes in-band: **maximize recall first, then precision — lost context is unrecoverable** (§5.6) — exactly the lossy dependency v3's commit-not-replay avoids.

### 9.4 L — Lifecycle: the headline confirmed with a section number (§6)
- **§6.1 "Lifecycle State Management" NEVER mentions idempotency, exactly-once, CAS, version, transactional outbox, event-sourcing, or compensation.** Its only framing axis is stateless-vs-stateful-vs-hybrid; "consistency" appears once as a hand-wave ("stateful designs introduce consistency and debugging challenges"). **"Durable" in §6.1 means *resumable*, not *transactional*** ("continue from prior work instead of treating every step as an isolated call"). This is the corpus-wide blind spot v3's L0a fills, now with a citation. **Caveat for v3's own framing:** the survey does not *debate* commit-vs-replay and lose — it never raises the question. v3 must frame idempotency as **a gap the corpus is blind to**, not a side it takes.
- **The "Stateful" label in Table 2 is unaudited** — 9 multi-agent + 3 issue-to-PR systems are tagged Stateful, defined merely as "stores operational state outside the prompt." No system is credited with a dedup/CAS/outbox mechanism. The crowd builds orchestration on persisted blobs without a correctness kernel — v3's suspicion holds with receipts.
- **The closest thing to a durable kernel in the whole corpus — Anthropic Managed Agents — is filed under *Observability* (§7.4), not Lifecycle.** It splits brain/hands/session, and on harness crash `wake(sessionId)` resumes "from the last event in the session log" (pets→cattle). But this is **event-sourced replay-resume, still replay-based, not commit/CAS-based** — adjacent to v3's outbox/ledger, not equivalent. The architectural irony (durable execution filed under O) is itself a sign the field has no settled home for it; v3 gives it one (L0a).
- **§6.3 enumerates exactly five multi-agent coordination patterns** = a checklist for v3's L4: (1) hierarchical (controller→subagents→integrate), (2) team (named specialists), (3) workflow (explicit stages), (4) fan-out (parallel diverse exploration), (5) graph composition (LangGraph/Hive). v3's L4 fan-in/correlation = fan-out + hierarchical.
- **§6.4 names v3's WF-1 abstraction: the "task runner"** — "a harness that manages scheduling, state persistence, retries, validation, and iterative refinement over a complete task lifecycle," stages issue→plan→generate→validate→human-review→PR. Adopt "task runner" as WF-1 vocabulary — **but note it bundles "retries" with zero dedup semantics** (§6.4 lists retries as a feature, never noting a retry without idempotency re-executes side effects).
- **Codex CLI is the survey's one pure replay example** (§6.2), at the *single-agent* level; the survey never debates replay-vs-commit as an *orchestration-kernel* choice. v3's bet sits above the level where the survey discusses replay at all.

### 9.5 O — Observability: promoted to first-class, with the inventory (§7)
- **§7 verbatim: O is "the first of the two layers our taxonomy promotes from Lifecycle Hooks to first-class status"** — external validation that v3 should lift the observe-seam to a named concern with cost/trace/reliability outputs.
- **Named inventory** (so v3 knows the field): tracing — Langfuse, Opik, Arize Phoenix, MLflow, **OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions** (de-facto standard), OpenLLMetry/OpenInference, **AgentSight** (eBPF, monitors from *outside* the process at the SSL boundary, <3% CPU, **uncircumventable by a compromised agent**), AgentTrace; ops — AgentOps, Laminar, **Watson** (surrogate-agent "cognitive observability" — recovers *why*); cost — TensorZero, Helicone (drop-in proxy), **FrugalGPT** (cascading, up to 98% cost cut), GPTCache (semantic cache). The **AgentSight out-of-process model** matters for v3: an observe-seam a compromised actor cannot tamper with belongs *below* the actor, in the kernel/runtime.
- **§7.4 reliability = Anthropic's four long-running failure modes** (one-shotting / premature completion / broken-environment-between-sessions / done-without-testing) + a **layered detection strategy** (rule-based for structural failures, statistical for drift, LLM-semantic for reasoning). **AgentFixer: 38% of failures are parsing errors "fully addressable by deterministic checks"** — supports gstack's deterministic-gate bet. v3's L0a ledger addresses "broken-environment-between-sessions" at the protocol level none of these reach.
- **§7.5 "the observability–evaluation gap"** (89% use observability, only 52% run offline eval) **closes by auto-generating regression tests from production failure traces** — "a single feedback loop." This is exactly v3's deferred-gate-into-regression, named. Plus the **harness-as-assumption** thesis (each component encodes an assumption about model weakness → need a meta-monitor of which interventions are "still load-bearing") = the §12.5 self-ablation problem.

### 9.6 V — Verification: the 5-stage lifecycle, fully substantiated (§8)
- **The root framing: "a reported score is a property of the model–harness *pair*, not the model"** (§8.1) — the academic root of "durable artifact over self-report": the harness is part of what is measured, so a self-reported score is structurally untrustworthy. **"Infrastructure noise masquerades as model failure"** (broken tools, stale context, non-reset sandboxes, flaky tests, unstable judges).
- **"The evaluator is a component *under test*, not an oracle"** (§8.5.3) — the single strongest line for v3's verify gate. Prescribes **layered graders**: deterministic checks for objective state, LLM judges for semantic/trajectory, human audits for high-risk — maps onto v3's allow/warn/block × declarative/packaged/process grid as **grader-type-per-risk-tier.**
- **The 5 stages give the `verify` gate its sub-structure:** Stage 2 *pre-execution readiness* = a real **FRONT gate** with three sub-checks (environment/sandbox reset; tool/context/permission consistency — "else the benchmark no longer measures the same action space"; **evaluator/grader versioned before use**) §8.3; Stage 4 *multi-level judgement* = a panel layered by **level not voter** — outcome / trajectory ("a correct answer with an unacceptable trajectory") / evaluator §8.5; Stage 5 *continuous regression* = the **deferred-gate-into-a-layered-suite** (unit-like for tool schemas, single-step, full-rollout, multi-turn sim) §8.6.
- **Regression must be re-triggered by *harness* changes, not just model changes** (§8.6.1) — "local improvements create global regressions because layers interact." For v3: **a gate/policy change must re-fire the deferred suite.** And **traces are primary evaluation data, not auxiliary debugging** (§8.4.2: record outputs, tool calls/results, state changes, context snapshots, errors, retries, recovery, tokens, latency, cost) — production traces *become* regression tests (§8.6.2). Direct backing for commit-log-as-truth feeding the suite.
- **Don't compress verify to one boolean** (outcome-only is "necessary but not sufficient"), **don't blind-reuse a strong model as judge** (position/verbosity/self-enhancement bias → version + bias controls), **don't rank on success rate alone** (expose a success–cost–latency frontier; gates carry cost/latency, not just correctness) §8.4.3/§8.5.

### 9.7 G — Governance: the constitution is a YAML *file*, the enforcement is *hooks* (§9)
- **"Declarative constitution" = the YAML config *file*; the runtime checks are "lifecycle hooks"** (§9.4). The **AutoHarness schema** gives v3's policy-config its field-list: *pipeline mode (core/standard/enhanced), risk-classification patterns, allowed+denied tool patterns, token-budget limits, audit-log destinations.* But position it consciously on a **three-point DSL spectrum**: free-form YAML (ambiguous) → structured policy DSL (Progent boolean predicates; Formal-LLM pushdown automata for ordering/required-gates; VeriSafeAgent) → hard-coded — "YAML admits ambiguity; formal specs demand expertise." **No portable standard schema exists** (§9.7) — v3 must define its own, MCP-analogously for future portability.
- **The four canonical hook points = v3's gate-placement spec** (§9.2): **H1** input guardrail (before LLM), **H2** action validation (before tool exec — *gstack's PreToolUse*), **H3** post-exec information-flow control (tool output → context), **H4** human-in-the-loop (*v3's DECISION_REQUEST*). HITL hooks have three named design dimensions — validation scope / alert richness / recurrence (allow-once vs allow-always) — directly parameterizing L3's DECISION_REQUEST payload. The **Android-permission warning** (17% read dialogs, 3% comprehend) argues for **rich, infrequent** decisions, not frequent habituated ones.
- **Credential-never-travels is the survey's exact pattern** (§9.1, Skyvern): secrets in a vault, only *placeholders* to the LLM, raw values substituted at the execution layer. Named *unsolved* part = **secret lifecycle over long-horizon sessions** (tokens expire/revoke mid-trajectory; renewed creds must stay outside model context) — a live v3 L7 risk. Identity reference model: **authenticated-delegation token chain** (South et al. extend OAuth2/OIDC with User-ID + Agent-ID + scoped Delegation Tokens = "verifiable accountability trail from human principal to agent action"); SAGA adds one-time keys + short-lived inter-agent tokens.
- **The replayable-audit-record field list = the paperclip-ledger spec** (§9.5, verbatim): "*trace identifier, principal identity, tool invocation, policy decision and version, execution result, resource cost, and an integrity hash over inputs and outputs.*" Survey's warning: "**few sign or hash records, leaving audit trails susceptible to log tampering by a compromised agent**" → **v3's ledger must hash/sign.** And **per-action vs trajectory-level detection** is a real split (per-action misses slow exfiltration spread across benign actions; trajectory-level — AgentAuditor, SentinelAgent temporal-graph — runs async over the log) → v3 = **inline per-commit gates + async ledger-scan.**
- **Stacked-hook interference is a named failure mode** (§9.2/§9.6): "an upstream sanitizer alters the signals a downstream detector relies on, leaving the pipeline weaker than either component alone" — v3's multi-gate L2 chains need explicit ordering/interaction tests, not naive layering. (This is the §11.3 harness-coupling problem at the gate level.)

### 9.8 Synthesis & open problems — the corrections that matter (§11–§12)
- **Capability–control: the survey confirms it is ONE axis *because* it threads the five levels** (§11.2, verbatim: "a design axis that links tool schemas, context policy, runtime permissions, identity, auditability, and human approval"). This **resolves** the tension in this study's §7: "one-config-surface-over-five-mechanism-levels" is the *correct* reading, not a contradiction — the survey itself treats the five as one axis precisely because the config threads them.
- **Frameworks→platforms, the precise definition** (§11.4): *frameworks* package local abstractions (agents, tools, memory, loops); ***platforms* add durable workspaces, managed sandboxes, identity, billing, observability, evaluation, governance, and human handoff across many runs/users.** The design question becomes "how do I operate a *fleet* of agents whose actions remain **inspectable and reversible** over time?" — **v3 as a *kernel* sits *under* the platform tier** (it is what makes actions inspectable-and-reversible: the commit log).
- **Handoff field-list, corrected count** (§12.4, verbatim): the rich handoff transfers, beyond a text summary, **nine named fields — intent, constraints, permissions, artifacts, provenance, budget state, risk level, trace history, unresolved decisions** — *plus* five **responsibility clauses**: *who authorized the action, which state was transferred, which evidence supports the plan, what the receiver may do, when control must return.* **Adopt as a two-section ContextPacket checklist** (payload fields + responsibility clauses). The earlier "nine-item" reading was right on the fields, but the five responsibility clauses are a second, distinct section v3's ContextPacket must carry.
- **Durable-artifact recovery, endorsed verbatim** (§12.2): "*recovery procedures that let an agent reconstruct missing state from durable artifacts rather than trusting its own compressed history.*" Direct academic backing for v3's commit-log-as-source-of-truth over compressed context — even though §5.6 mis-files the mechanism under context management, §12.2 (open problems) states the goal in v3's exact terms.
- **L9 has no prior art anywhere — confirmed across §9–§13:** no fuzzy or external-correlation mechanism appears in governance, synthesis, or open problems; every handoff/identity/correlation mechanism surveyed is exact/explicit. The standout open gap stands.
- **§12.5 self-ablating harness, sharpened:** "every wrapper/verifier/permission gate encodes an assumption about model weakness; as models improve, interventions must be **re-estimated, not assumed beneficial**" (Anthropic example: context resets useful for one model became dispensable for a stronger one). v3 has no "is this gate still load-bearing?" mechanism — capture as: **gates should be ablatable / shadow-testable**, and the O-layer should meta-monitor which interventions still pay.

## Caveats

- **README-coded, primary-label, snapshot.** The corpus measures *advertised* capability from public docs, with one primary layer per project and a current-snapshot count. It is a map of the *published, open-sourced, documented* ecosystem — it under-counts capabilities buried in frameworks (hence the low *primary* C=9, though §5.5 shows a rich standalone-memory market behind it) and behind commercial walls (hence thin O/G). Counts are directional, not precise; treat layer *ordering* (L≫V≈E≫O≈G≈T≫C) as the durable signal, not the integers. **Corpus-count inconsistency:** the companion-page aggregate table sums to ~138 primary assignments, while the paper body cites "over 100" (§6) and "148+" (§7) — it is a *living catalog* (Awesome-Agent-Harness) counted at different times. Cite the *ordering*, not a specific total.
- **This study had two passes.** §1–§8 were written from the companion page; §9 (the deep-dive addendum) was mined from the full 71-page PDF by five layer-sliced subagents. Where the two disagree, §9 wins (it read the source) — notably the C-market correction (finding 7) and the §6.1 idempotency-blindness, which the companion page could only assert and the full paper confirms with section numbers.
- **No depth, no battle-testing.** A taxonomy paper offers boundaries and vocabulary, not mechanisms or `file:line`. Every mechanism-level claim in this study is imported from the *prior* studies (paperclip audit, honcho memory, gastown gate-bead, vibe-kanban observe-seam, Temporal kernel); the survey only tells us how to *name and bound* them. Do not cite the survey for an implementation choice — cite it for a boundary.
- **Academic framing bias.** The survey's instinct to fold durability into "state estimation / context management" (open #2) is a memory-research lens; v3's transactional-kernel lens is a systems lens. Where they disagree on *where durability lives*, v3's separation is deliberate — note it rather than absorb the survey's framing.
- **Figures.** Five figures analyzed (teaser = the three-phase arc; taxonomy = the E/T/C/L pillars under O, over V, on G; taxonomy_tree = the full subcategory leaves reproduced in §2; timeline = ReAct-era → tool/multi-agent → harness-discipline, 2022→2026; construction_protocol = the corpus coding pipeline). Local copies under `/tmp/llm-harness-figs/` are ephemeral; re-fetch from `picrew.github.io/LLM-Harness/assets/figs/` if needed. The timeline's specific dated system attributions were read at era/layer granularity to avoid misattribution from the dense small-type layout.
- **This closes the divergence/research phase as a survey, fittingly.** The first study (omnigent) and this last one are both *meta-harnesses*; the eleven in between are the systems the survey would code. ETCLOVG is the frame the whole series implicitly built — having an external group publish it is the cleanest possible confirmation that the series was carving at real joints.
